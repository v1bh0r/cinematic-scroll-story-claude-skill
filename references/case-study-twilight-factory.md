# Case study: the Twilight Factory (a software-modernization landing page, September 2026)

This is one worked example, for calibration. **Do not reuse its world** (dusk, factory, skyline, "light
means people"). Reuse how it was derived and what the feedback taught.

## Message → world

- **Site:** a service that modernizes legacy software with AI agents and engineers.
- **Promise:** point it at the legacy system your team won't touch; it works through the night and
  keeps a person in the loop. The operator's framing: "a dark factory, but with a human in the loop".
- **Method** (from a published playbook): define the target, certificate, promotion policy,
  prerequisites, a workflow built for the codebase, the pilot.
- **Proof:** two published case studies with links, and a partner-network tier badge.
  All of it sat below the film, and the film itself carried no numbers.
- **World:** a factory at dusk on a lake shore, facing a city of old towers across the water.
- **Rule:** *light means people.*
  - The factory is lit while people set it up.
  - One warm window stays lit because the decisions stay yours.
  - The city's towers light up as the systems come back to people who can work in them.

## Beats

| # | Shot | What happens | Copy (gist) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Across the lake; the camera arrives already moving | Opening push; the factory lit | Hero: "The Twilight Factory." |
| 1 | High, toward the city | Six towers labelled as systems with what's wrong; Invoicing goes dark, its dependants flicker, a piece falls, "Invoicing · down again", then it comes back as if nothing happened | "They still run the business. When one goes down, you find out how few people know how it works." |
| 2–6 | Inside the hall, station by station | 01 the target (hologram); 02 the certificate gate (one in five changes fails, drops onto a chute, labelled with its reason); 03 promotion (one change waits under the lit booth, the person signs it off, a warm flash, a close push-in); 04 prerequisites (four supply panels fill); 05 the workflow (a high angle as the line re-tools) | One step per beat |
| 7 | Out through the output portal to the pilot line | The pilot runs | The pilot: measure real cost first |
| 8 | Wide | The factory lights go out; machinery glows; one window stays lit ("Still your call") | It runs unattended; the decisions stay yours |
| 9 | Toward the city | Towers rebuilt one by one; each label flips from problem to outcome; long slow push | "One by one, your systems come back to your people." |

## Feedback that changed it, and the general lesson

| Operator feedback | Lesson |
|---|---|
| "Jitters when it starts and plays back" | Spring the camera; fix the canvas size; no per-frame layout reads |
| "Contrast needs revisiting" | Scrims per beat type; check every beat, not just the hero |
| "Transitions get stuck at an angle that shows nothing" | Settle to the next shot; hold 45% then ease |
| "Target the heart, not just the mind… let the user bask" | Events on clocks after arrival; slower moves; three acts |
| "Buildings aren't obviously in distress at first — that's how legacy is" | Problems look fine from afar; reveal them with labels and an event |
| "Lights go out, then lights come on: lights where?" | One rule, and say whose light it is every time |
| "Talk about the systems, not the city" | The metaphor stays on screen; the words name reality |
| "Add labels: security, frameworks, modernization" | Callouts carry the specific pain, then the specific outcome |
| "Low-res on Chrome" | Slope-based calibration; verify in Chrome at laptop sizes |
| "The pan out at 04 then back in is unexpected" | Don't leave a set mid-sequence; stage the step inside |
| Script review (PAS/PAIPS) → chosen: agitate, tension, camera, sound | Show the failure; put a failure and a human decision in the middle; a hook, shot variety, a motivated exit; opt-in sound |

## Constraints that were specific to that site (examples of what to ask for)

- A live analytics beacon fired on every load, so previews were made from a neutralised copy only.
- A confidential reference client never appeared; the demo used a fictional one with altered numbers.
- Publishing used an allowlisted `gh-pages` branch, pushed only on request, and an `llms.txt`
  that described the film honestly as an illustration.
